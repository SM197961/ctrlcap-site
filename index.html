Where it goes wrong

Four places a troubled loan gets worse.

The same four, at almost every lender, usually at the moment the book outgrows the people who built it.

01

The default notice goes out, and then nothing

A loan matures or misses a payment, the letter goes out, and the file waits. Nobody owns the next step, so the borrower sets the pace. Every week without a plan costs interest, equity, and leverage you will want later.

02

The file lives in three inboxes

Payoff demands, borrower promises, inspection photos, and counsel's advice sit in email threads held by whoever happened to be on them. When a dispute, a bankruptcy, or an investor question arrives, the record has to be rebuilt from memory.

03

Investors hear about it last

The watch list is a spreadsheet, the status is in someone's head, and the capital partner finds out a loan is in trouble from the quarterly report. That is the conversation that costs a fund its next raise.

04

Servicing was built for loans that perform

Payments, draws, and payoffs run fine. Extensions, forbearance, modifications, and foreclosure do not, because the process was never written for them. So each one is handled from scratch, by the most senior person available, at the worst possible time.

A troubled loan is not a legal problem yet. It is an operations problem with a clock on it.

What we do

Three pieces of work, for the loans that need a senior hand.

Each one can stand alone. Most engagements start with the first and end with the third.

Triage · Review

Portfolio triage and loan-by-loan review

Every loan on the watch list gets the same look: where it stands, what the collateral is worth today, what the documents allow, and which path, from cure to foreclosure, protects the most value. You get a ranked list and a plan for each file, not a memo.

  • Watch-list review against documents, payment history, and current collateral value
  • A recommended path per loan: cure, extension, forbearance, modification, payoff, or enforcement
  • Exposure summary by borrower, asset type, and maturity
  • The gaps in the file that have to be closed before any path is safe to take
Workouts · Restructuring

Workout and restructuring plans

Once the path is chosen, I run it or help your team run it: the borrower conversation, the terms, the paper, and the cadence. Firm, fair, and documented, because the file you build during a workout is the file you will stand on if it fails.

  • Forbearance, extension, and modification terms sized to what the collateral and the borrower can actually carry
  • Borrower communication that is direct, on a schedule, and written down
  • Coordination with counsel, trustees, receivers, and brokers through foreclosure and REO
  • A decision record for every file: what was offered, what was accepted, and why
Servicing · Operations

Servicing setup and operations

A troubled-loan desk your team can run after I leave: who does what, in what order, on what timeline, with reporting your investors and your credit committee can read before they ask. Built inside the servicing platform you already have.

  • Default, maturity, and watch-list procedures with owners and clocks
  • Queues for extensions, forbearance, modifications, payoffs, and foreclosure, with aging and escalation
  • Investor and committee reporting on specials that assembles itself from the file
  • Cleanup of the servicing operation underneath: payoffs, extensions, and reconciliations that have drifted
